# wedgie Training app: timing and RAM (plan M3.9)

The Training app of `wedgie/app/`, with `jiro_core.mpy` of the same commit (18,588 bytes, CI's sha256 `4968acd4c70243b651607176d41d9572ac83357eed8d0cb3272c7c0167f51de8`). Round E1's first two tries ran builds of 18,180 bytes, CI's sha256 `5867fff86fa3ba7bd774b87d997c187736466925114abd49b28d07b314ff548f`, and its third the code review's, 18,556 bytes, CI's sha256 `8d0e9314bd59c9de675e9539f57dcffc0483984c7c0ea2003918f886626c5c1a`: the modules the `wedgie-app` artifacts carry (a build in another directory can differ, `wedgie/README.md`). Tools: MicroPython v1.29.0 (commit 0fd6c573) and its qemu port for the MICROBIT board (Cortex-M0, 236 KB heap), qemu-system-arm 8.2.2, arm-none-eabi-gcc 13.2.1, rustc 1.98.0. The wedgie's own numbers are round E1's, below.

### Refactor and fixes (2026-11-10)

`wedgie/app/jiro.py` and `jiro_app.py`, refactored in the wedgie repository, then four changes of behaviour, each its own commit, the checks that came with them, and what a review of those commits found. `jiro_core.mpy` and `jiro_levels.bin` are the same bytes as before. **This build has not run on a wedgie:** every number below is qemu's, and round E1's, below, are the build before's.

- **The refactor** changes no behaviour: the play test's transcript, its journals, every screen's text, the level lines and the stored bests, is the same line for line as the build before's. The app's numbers are `const()`, which mpy-cross writes where they are used, so a name costs no RAM and the tick no lookup; `render` owns the two status arrays, which `ticks` swapped and wrote back; `finish` is the summary, with the best and the journal's lines in calls of their own; `jiro.py`'s `heap` returns what it measured, so no dict of stages goes through the app; the shadow's size comes from `jiro_core`.
- **The summary** put the score, the best and "best not saved: flash full" on one line: up to 39 characters on a screen whose lines hold 28, a screen no round reached. The message now has a fourth line, in red. A best that a failed store left out of the save was stored only by a later new best; while a store has failed, every level's end now stores the bests, new best or not.
- **The overlay** showed a target's whole percentage: Ring's 7,650 basis points as 76 % and Titan's 8,150 as 81 %. It gives them as 76.50 % and 81.50 %. The summary said "1 lives".
- **The schedule** was absolute for the whole level, so a level that had stalled ran every tick it was behind by, one after the other. A tick up to one tick late still runs at once on its own due time, which keeps the level's 15 ticks a second through a slow tick (the 90.3 ms of the sixth try is 23.6 ms late); a tick later than that starts the schedule again from itself. The review found the first version of this change waiting on, for up to 9 minutes, when the clock jumped by more than 2²⁹ µs inside the wait's sleep: round E1's guard against a misread due time, which that version kept at the wait's start only. The wait now reads the clock's range each time round, after its sleep too, which is where the app spends most of a tick.
- **The drawing** pushed the changed cells, then drew the entities and pushed them: for the time of a capture's push, its rows showed without the boss, a minion or the player that stood on them. The changed cells now go out once the entities are drawn, the same calls in another order.
- **The heap in a tick.** The review also found what no check had seen, in the build before as in the first of these commits: the screen's methods were bound once at the start, and a bound method called with `fill_rect`'s five arguments takes a 24-byte array for them from the heap (`py/objboundmeth.c`), for the boss, each live minion, the ring and the player, 3 to 6 times a tick. Under the play test's lock that array goes to the C stack, as a frame over 44 bytes does. The methods are now called on the screen, which takes nothing, and `--allocations` counts it. The stack's size is read when the app starts, as before the refactor, so that lending the stack's block to `save.store` touches the stack no more than it did.

## wedgie (round E1)

**First try** (wedgie RP2040 at 125 MHz, firmware 0.3.76, MicroPython 1.29.0): "it works but it's buggy". Level 1 froze in play, about 2 minutes after plugging in, while the owner drew a line to finish a capture: the picture stopped, no button did anything. No log. The white player dot was hard to see on the red trail.

- **The freeze was not reproduced.** On qemu, about 50,000 random and adversarial play ticks over 117 levels, 302 captures up to 84 % of the field, C-stack and 64 KB-stack canaries, framebuffer guards and every journal against native replay found no hard fault, hang or exception (`step` 344 bytes, the renderer's exports at most 2.7 KB of C stack with the app's depth, `show_rect` always on the screen).
- **What did hang on qemu,** and is fixed: the tick wait read a due time more than 2²⁹ µs late as up to 9 minutes ahead (`ticks_diff`'s range) and slept that long, with the picture still and the buttons dead. It needs ticks far slower than a wedgie's, so it is not likely E1's freeze; a due time read more than one tick ahead now runs at once, and the due time is a running one, so `ticks_add`'s delta stays small (the absolute one would have overflowed at tick 8,054, past any level's 2,700). The play test's clock jump covers it: the first build hung there.
- **Also changed:** the summary screen is drawn before the USB output, which can block 500 ms a print when a host holds the port open unread, so a level that ends on a capture shows its summary at once instead of seconds later; presses made in the level's last moments and the summary's first 0.5 s no longer skip it; the play loop binds the screen's methods once instead of allocating them every tick; the player is drawn larger, in a black ring.
- **For the next try,** if it freezes again: whether the claim was near the level's target (a clear), whether the screen changed after 30 s, USB power or battery, and the output of `python3 wedgie.py debug` afterwards.

**Second try** (round E1's build, e6198d3): after install the level select showed level 1 of 16; green showed "Jiro training / memory allocation failed, allocating 1148 bytes". The first build had started and played level 1 on the same wedgie.

- **The allocation** was the level file's: the app opened `jiro_levels.bin` at every level start, and a file on the wedgie's littlefs is a 1,148-byte object, 124 bytes and a 1,024-byte cache (MicroPython 1.29.0's rp2 port mounts it with `progsize=256`; `extmod/vfs_lfsx_file.c`). On qemu the e6198d3 build's largest free block after start was 747 bytes (the first build's 3,279), and the wedgie's heap is more fragmented than the old play test's ballast, which left the free bytes in a few blocks and served the files from objects of a few bytes.
- **Fixed:** `jiro.py` allocates the level buffer, reads the bests and keeps the level file open from the start, so a level start seeks and reads into the buffer; nothing in the play path allocates more than a short string. `save.store`, which opens a file too, gets the core's stack's block, idle between levels, which the app frees for it and takes back. The level select shows, and USB prints, the free heap and largest free block at each stage (`run`, `buf`, `imp`, `lcd`, `app`, `sel`, `lvl`).
- **The play test** now models it: a 1,148-byte file object per `open()`, small holes in the free bytes, every block over 1 KB filled once the app has started, and two levels in a row. The e6198d3 build fails it at its first level start with the wedgie's error; this build passes. An ad-hoc run with the heap locked over each tick's step and drawing, the player's ring included, passed: a tick allocates nothing on the heap.

**Third try** (the code review's build, merged as 284fc61): "everything went fine from level 1 to 8, I got a freeze at level 9".

- **The cause** was the wedgie firmware's interrupts, which no test modelled. Its `power.py` registers a hard IRQ on every key, `Pin.irq(_touch, Pin.IRQ_FALLING, hard=True)`, which records the time of the last press for the battery's idle sleep (read in firmware 0.3.77, the version wedgie.dev serves, from its `.mpy` files disassembled with MicroPython v1.29.0's `mpy-tool.py`, not run). A hard IRQ runs its Python callback at once, on the stack of the code it interrupts. `call_on_stack.S` moved the main stack onto the app's 64 KB buffer for the core, so a press during a level start or a step ran `_touch` there. MicroPython checks the C stack's depth on every call, as `stack_top − sp` against the 8 KB limit; on the buffer, far below the C stack, the check failed and raised a `RuntimeError`. The rp2 port gives hard IRQs no stack of their own (`MICROPY_STACK_SIZE_HARD_IRQ` is 0), and `mp_irq_dispatch` printed the exception; printing checks the stack again, so a second exception escaped the handler, past its `gc_unlock()` and `mp_sched_unlock()` (`shared/runtime/mpirq.c`, `py/obj.c`). With the garbage collector and the scheduler left locked, every allocation fails and no scheduled callback runs: the picture stops, the buttons do nothing and USB stops answering. A press has to land in the core's few milliseconds of a 66.7 ms tick, which is rare: that fits a freeze in level 9 after eight good levels, and the first try's freeze during a capture, the longest step. The firmware's other timers are soft, and their callbacks cannot run during the core; it has no watchdog, and it sleeps only on battery, after 20 s without a press.
- **Reproduced on qemu** with a stand-in for the key IRQ, dispatched from the qemu port's SysTick by MicroPython's own `mp_irq_dispatch`: the code review's build froze at the first call that interrupted the core ("Uncaught exception in IRQ callback handler", then no level ended), in heap layouts of 0 to 44 holes.
- **Fixed:** `call_on_stack.S` runs the core on the Cortex-M0's process stack, set to the buffer, and leaves the main stack where it was. Exception handlers always run on the main stack, so an interrupt stays on the C stack, where `_touch` runs as anywhere else, and puts only its 32-byte exception frame on the buffer. A panic in the core goes back to the main stack before it raises. Masking interrupts during the core, the first fix tried, also stops the freeze, but a panic then leaves them masked, which stops the clock (on qemu, `ticks_ms` stood still after a panic), and it stops qemu's SysTick-driven clock during the core, which hid the core's time from the play test and the vector run.
- **The play test** gained a `--hard-irq` mode, which CI runs on the firmware `build-qemu.sh irqsim` builds with that stand-in: every SysTick calls a callback like `_touch` as a hard IRQ. This build passes it, with 44 to 47 of about 17,400 calls interrupting the core and none failing, in heap layouts of 0 to 44 holes; the code review's build fails it at the first such call, and the masking fix fails it with no call in the core. Without it the play test and the 75 vectors give this build the code review's results, and its times within 3 µs (stack peaks: `start` 62,784, `step` 344, `replay` 44,072, one exception frame over 44,040).

**Fourth try** (the freeze's fix, 2026-11-09): "all the 16 level works on the wedgie, fully tested and finished all of them." After level 16's clear the app went back to level 16's level select; at the owner's request it now shows "Congrats! visit jiro-ikusu.win" there, and green goes to level 1 (a scripted run of the app's level flow on qemu shows it, and main's app goes back to level 16).

The app now takes its run nonce from `os.urandom(32)`, and the level select read `sel 15.5/11.4`.
